# SkyCell integrated flight experience

The ascent experience originated from `origin/main` (`16d83f3`), then developed through ascent, cinematic and flight-story revisions. The current `codex/skycell-scroll-flight` branch continues integrated-flight checkpoint `f4e48e9` and the selected live Three.js direction: the flight, hardware inspection and recording form one continuous scene. The [integrated brief](integrated-flight-brief.md) is the current design and acceptance contract. The validated archive parser, replay clock and advanced dashboard remain foundations.

## Page and information

Manrope, deep atmospheric blue, white and a restrained signal cyan form one visual system. Large direct headlines describe the current object or question. The permanent illustration caption, floating chapter labels, hero award, separate white field-story epilogue and footer have been removed. The final chapter retains source links and the accessible **Flight notes & evidence** dialog. Removing the footer leaves the final full-height chapter inside its sticky scene rather than pushing its large text under the fixed header.

Three bounded heading treatments are implemented as isolated query-selected comparisons: **Lift** (`?headings=1`) reveals lines through a rising mask; **Depth** (`?headings=2`) combines scroll entry with pointer-responsive line offsets and tilt; **Signal** (`?headings=3`) sweeps brighter text across the heading as its chapter arrives. Their inputs and variant switching are verified. Depth is the recommendation, not an automatic selection: the base URL retains the accepted heading treatment pending Sharon's choice. Static/reduced-motion mode uses ordinary readable text.

The continuous sequence has eight chapters:

1. **Flight:** one balloon leads a denser, suspended payload, with distinct movement and a changing tether angle.
2. **Ascent:** the camera follows the same moving vehicle past fixed cloud volumes as Earth recedes.
3. **Payload:** approach the same foam enclosure, preserving its identity and antenna; an inspection link opens the actual build photograph.
4. **Inside:** lift the lid and separate interpreted sensing, computation, radio and power assemblies. This is a technical cutaway, not an event that occurred during flight.
5. **Connection:** use the antenna and spatial signal explanation to present the 70 km, 915 MHz result alongside the limited number of received payload packets.
6. **Power:** return to the relevant component to explain startup brownouts and the shorter operating window.
7. **Recording:** reveal the event tracker’s 125 actual fixes and time-linear estimates between them. Scrolling moves the same balloon/payload along the complete partial-archive path, reveals travelled segments and orbits the camera; amber joins distinguish reception gaps.
8. **Resolve:** return to the complete vehicle, the four-student result, project source and flight notes.

There is no separate three-tab hardware menu. The [optimized project photographs](hardware-gallery-assets.md) remain useful in the normal-flow fallback; inspection dialogs use their original JPEG evidence. The actual launch photograph retains its two-balloon train and factual caption. Reduced motion, unavailable WebGL and scene errors use `sky-story-static`: all eight chapters remain in normal reading order, with photographs, a static recording profile and manual controls.

## Flight, camera and inspection

`flightDynamics.ts` provides the shared wind-driven pose from physical ascent progress and the parent’s visible scene time. Wind forces drive both the envelope and an analytic damped suspension response; the payload follows a lagged point on the curved route and overshoots independently. An instance-owned `ScrollSuspension` adds a bounded, damped payload offset opposite the balloon's lateral screen travel during operated camera crossings. It responds to touch, keyboard, anchor navigation and reverse scroll, settles after the crossing, and freezes with a zero timestep while paused or hidden. Its maximum relative correction is **0.90 m**, with integration substeps up to 1/120 s; this affects suspension only and does not add another camera filter. Ordinary-flight geometry projects the combined response onto an exact **2.947 m** nominal taut attachment length. The local route rises **28 m**, while environmental altitude independently progresses **8→30 km**. These are authored visualization coordinates and a reduced-order performance model, not measured rig dimensions or a full aerodynamic simulation. During the recording chapter, the rig is repositioned onto the projected archived/estimated path.

The camera follows the actual sampled pose before approaching the enclosure for a purposeful inspection. It no longer keeps a stationary train and moves it between screen positions. The original exterior GLB remains the reference; code-authored cavity walls, circuit roles, wiring and a lifted lid extend it for the cutaway. Hidden surfaces and exact interior arrangement are interpreted from photographs. Shared reusable anchors keep suspension, camera targets and inspection parts consistent. The cord fades to 7% during the technical inspection range, approximately narrative progress .25–.82, then returns for ordinary flight; it does not dominate the cutaway or recording. The scene does not assert that the sealed enclosure opened in flight.

One Framer spring filters narrative scroll. The renderer publishes its actual presented progress through a MotionValue for HTML composition and interaction state, without a second continuous filter. After scrolling while paused, a bounded adaptive bridge reconnects the held pose: **0.3 + 0.75 × progress gap seconds**. A small gap settles quickly; a whole-journey jump gets more travel time. The parent-owned clock excludes time spent hidden, offscreen, paused or inside a modal dialog, and resets the first active timestep on waking. After finite loading transitions finish, the demand-scheduled Canvas requests continuous frames only while the visible scene is performing. Recording phase, vehicle position, travelled trace and HTML readings share presented scroll progress.

The GLTF pair loads in parallel, while model, HDR and photographic Earth loaders have separate nested Suspense boundaries. A texture-free `ProgressiveSky` supplies the physical spherical horizon without waiting on maps or HDR; fallback illumination remains available while environment lighting loads. The Canvas intentionally fades in over **220 ms after the actual rig mounts and completes three warm frames**, independently of Earth/HDR arrival. A rig poster is used only in static mode, so the live page cannot crossfade a poster balloon into a second model balloon. Once Earth imagery resolves, only its final display pass fades over the physical sky for 450 ms. This bounded asset-loading transition has its own clamped frame age and requests frames until complete, even if flight movement is paused; the actors and copy remain held. The cached Earth shading target is not recomputed for opacity alone. Static poster fixtures pass `fadeIn={false}`, so their four-frame captures remain fully opaque without needing a live scene clock.

## Shared environment and assets

The key light, wrapped-latex approximation, Earth, atmosphere and near clouds use one sun direction. The oriented Poly Haven pure-noon HDR supplies PMREM lighting. The key follows the payload’s world position while retaining that sun direction, so inspection detail does not lose its lighting when the vehicle travels.

Earth uses kilometres, a **6,371 km** radius and a fixed local east/up/north basis beginning at **42°N, 71°W**. The observer moves over the sphere along a bounded **66 km east / 18 km north** concept path, preserving radial altitude; the shader does not merely scroll a geographic texture under a stationary eye. NASA June global maps blend into georeferenced regional MODIS imagery from **2024-06-21**. A bounded local patch from NASA GIBS **Landsat/WELD's annual 30 m dataset, time `2000-12-01`**, supplies genuine higher-resolution coastal, city and terrain detail rather than upscaling MODIS. It covers **−71.6° to −69.8° longitude / 41.3° to 43.1° latitude**, feathering over 0.10° into the broader map. Separate global clouds remain excluded inside the regional patch. All these historical images provide geographic context, not 2025 flight-day imagery or weather. One 4096²/2048² detail variant is selected per mount and loaded independently after the base map; failure retains the broader map. An Earth-specific error boundary also keeps the physical sky and live vehicle available if base imagery fails.

Near clouds are seven fixed world-space volumes submitted in one instanced draw. A deterministic, locally baked **32³ single-channel density field (32 KiB)** supplies six bounded samples per ray, with shared sunlight, first-density depth and hardware occlusion. No cloud mesh is positioned or scaled in camera space. Cloud banks are placed below the initial vehicle to preserve the opening reading area. Their opacity fades between **12.5 and 18 km**, leaving hardware inspection clear. The old generated cirrus bitmap has been removed from runtime; its [generation record](flight-cloud-asset.md) is retained as history.

Earth shades into a cached linear HalfFloat target at the actual canvas pixel ratio, capped by a **2048-pixel longest edge desktop / 1024 compact**, with matching aspect and projection. This replaces the enlarged 1024/640-CSS-pixel background. The cache still ignores camera translation below **2 m** and approximately quarter-target-pixel rotation/projection changes; physical ascent, geographic travel, visible lens changes, sunlight or target changes refresh it. Foreground clouds retain genuine per-frame parallax. Canvas DPR is capped at **1.25**, and map anisotropy at 8× or the hardware limit. At that DPR a 1440×900 viewport produces an 1800×1125 target, about **15.45 MiB**; 390×844 produces a bounded 473×1024 target, about **3.70 MiB**. Earth-map RGBA allocation with mipmaps, including the detail patch, is estimated at **224 / 96 MiB** desktop/compact before targets, models, HDR and PMREM. These are allocation estimates, not measured total GPU memory.

The two GLBs, global NASA maps, one selected regional/detail variant each and HDR total **13,149,799 bytes desktop / 6,019,005 bytes compact**. The optional detail map contributes **5,681,120 / 1,161,924 bytes** of those totals. Code, font, archive photographs and two fallback layers are additional. The layers add **30,624 bytes**. The procedural density field has no media download; its bounded read-only CPU bytes are reused while each mounted cloud pass owns its GPU texture. All owned geometries, materials and render targets have explicit disposal; loader-cached model geometry and base imagery remain shared, while the optional detail texture is instance-owned. See [asset provenance](asset-credits.md).

## Recording and mission limits

The recording geometry preserves one point per actual observation, projected into a local east/north/up presentation with **0.5 scene units per kilometre on every axis**. Altitude is absolute. The showcase now joins the complete available interval: positions and altitude between recorded fixes are **linear estimates in time**, while amber joins explicitly mark reception gaps longer than **60 seconds**. No source rows are added or changed, and no launch/landing positions are extrapolated before or after the available interval. Original measured fixes remain distinct points. Between narrative progress **.815 and .95**, scrolling advances archive time, moves the same vehicle along the estimated/recorded path, brightens the travelled trace and updates UTC/altitude. The camera's scroll-driven orbit and subtle ground projection expose its three-dimensional form. The showcase no longer has a range slider. Reduced-motion/no-WebGL fallback retains the static profile and previous/next actual-fix controls; the advanced dashboard keeps its established replay controls and gap semantics.

SkyCell established an approximately **70 km LoRa / Meshtastic link at 915 MHz**, with an approximately **30 km** mission altitude. Python handled NMEA GPS, environmental readings and reconnection; the ground work included RTL-SDR **30 MHz–1.5 GHz at 2.4 MS/s**. Only a few payload packets were received. Startup brownouts required a power-bank workaround that shortened operating time. Range success is not a claim of continuous telemetry.

The separate **APEX-2-T / SondeHub event-tracker archive** contains **125 observations**, June 21, 2025 **18:11:06–19:17:21 UTC**, with a **29,510 m** peak in the partial recording. It is neither live nor SkyCell’s 915 MHz mesh packet stream. The flight-notes dialog preserves that distinction, the actual two-balloon event configuration and the cutaway’s interpretation.
