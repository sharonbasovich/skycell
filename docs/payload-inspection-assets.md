# Payload inspection assembly

The live payload close-up augments the original exterior GLB with an explanatory
cutaway. It is authored as Three.js geometry in
`src/experience/scene/PayloadReveal.tsx`; it adds no downloaded model, generated
photograph, external font texture, or paid asset.

The reference photographs are the unchanged project originals
`public/gallery/1000004204.jpg` (enclosure) and
`public/gallery/1000004195.jpg` (electronics on the workbench). The latter shows
a green computer board with metal connectors and a finned heat sink, a blue
converter board with terminal blocks and cylindrical components, a small pale
board, colored wiring, and smaller connected electronics. These visible material
and shape cues inform the modeled assembly.

The four displayed roles—sensing, compute, radio, and power—describe the confirmed
project system. Board placement, relative dimensions, traces, generic chips,
connector detail and the assignment of visible shapes to these roles are
interpretations for explanation. They are not scans or a measured reconstruction
of the packaged interior. No model numbers, chip identifiers, invented readings,
or unseen manufacturer claims are added.

During the hardware chapter, the cap, original paper labels, suspension washer,
tube and recovery loop lift together. A hollow EPS wall/floor assembly replaces
the original solid core; four circuit groups rise in sequence. During the power
chapter, the power group alone remains emphasized. The parts reassemble before
the archive/wide flight view. This opening is a technical inspection device, not
a claim that the enclosure opened during flight.

The moving suspension anchor is exported to the flight rig so its visible cord
continues to meet the actual recovery loop. The closed exterior and cached GLTF
geometry/textures remain owned by the surrounding train. New detail is merged by
material per module and is hidden during the wide flight. The factory disposes
only its new geometry and materials.

Root owns browser and GPU verification. Unit checks cover the narrative staging,
the recovery-loop anchor throughout opening/reversal, and resource ownership.
